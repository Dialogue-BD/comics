# Reverse-side question writing

Write six questions that move from concrete observation to personal and imaginative language. Use this progression unless the category makes a nearby variation more natural:

1. **Recognition or classification:** identify, name, group, or select items on the card.
2. **Attributes or comparison:** compare size, usefulness, cost, health, difficulty, or another category-relevant quality.
3. **Personal experience:** ask what the learner uses, likes, has seen, or has done.
4. **Use, routine, place, or process:** ask where, when, how, or why an item is used.
5. **Extension:** elicit related vocabulary not pictured or connect the category to the learner's context.
6. **Choice or imagination:** use a plausible preference, plan, role, or hypothetical.

Keep the language suitable for broad A2–B1 conversation practice. A question may contain two linked clauses, as in the source deck, but it must remain speakable and fit the reverse.

Avoid:

- six versions of “Which one do you like?”;
- culture-, income-, family-, health-, or ability-based assumptions;
- trivia that requires specialist knowledge;
- questions with a single obvious factual answer when discussion is possible;
- invented facts about the generated image;
- more than about 160 characters per numbered item.

End every numbered item with a question mark. Use ordinary ASCII hyphens when a hyphen is necessary.
