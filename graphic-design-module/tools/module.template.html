Teach Through The Visual

Teach visual communication as a useful life skill. Students may need to discuss a school slide, a family invitation, a community notice, a sign, a social post, or a work project. They do not need to be freelancers.

Begin with the graphical elements, explore how design principles organise them, then use the foundation finale to put graphic design and visual communication together. Each concept now opens a five-part design discovery: inspect examples, reveal subtype/property names, explore pictured vocabulary, change one design choice, and judge an everyday example. Then switch to the English mission. All parts are freely accessible. Every mission remains freely accessible. Every guided mission follows the same seven-step rhythm. Begin at the learning map; all seven graphical elements and eight principles are visible there. A mission’s “All 15 concepts” link returns to the complete foundation map.

  1. Observe. Compare a visible change, then reveal the animated word and hear its definition. The evidence question is optional.
  2. Understand. Hear a short conversation or voice message and identify the main point. Replay a speech bubble or open the transcript as needed.
  3. Check. Explore the meaning diagram and animated sentence pattern before the optional quiz, or collect useful details from a listening clip.
  4. Build. Assemble a model sentence. The check compares with the supplied model, not every possible grammatical sentence.
  5. Say. Practise the model aloud, point to the evidence, then change one detail. This is optional; no checkbox or voice grade is required.
  6. Discuss. Ask for missing information before recommending; connect the reply to the actual answer. For a listening mission, give a response or analysis.
  7. Pair work. Optionally try the new situation with a classroom partner: A suggests and explains; B asks a useful question. Swap roles. Solo learners can explore the example or finish.

Feedback popups explain specific evidence. Completion celebrates reaching the end. Every step button is clickable, and Back/Next always work without passing a challenge. Check answer is separate. Retry popups have a Continue option. The sound icon mutes narration and celebrations. Finishing requires no writing or speaking submission.

A2: short output and fuller frames. B1: connect a detail and a reason. B2: qualify interpretations, explain tradeoffs, and invite a response. Teacher feedback is especially useful during optional classroom speaking practice. The optional reference library retains design tools, sandboxes, and career examples.
