🏛️ Large Group Architecture: Solving the "Spectator Problem"

In a standard debate, 6 debaters speak while 30+ students sit passively. To ensure 100% active participation across a large class, structure the room into 3 functional tiers:

1. Stage Debaters (6–8 Students)

3 Govt (PM, Minister, Whip) & 3 Opp (LO, Deputy, Whip). Deliver the speeches at the podium facing the room.

2. Bench Caucus Pods (8–14 Students)

Research and strategy caucus. Each bench supports their speakers with Rajshahi evidence and drafts 15s POIs.

3. Student Jury & Adjudicators (All Others)

Audience jury members track clashes on paper and take a before/after shift vote on who won the motion.

⏱️ Phases 1 & 2: Setup & Team Caucus (15m)
  • Select Topic (2m): Click "🎲 Random Topic" or browse categories. Read the motion aloud in English and Bengali.
  • Form Benches (2m): Split room down the middle (Left = Government, Right = Opposition). Have each side pick their 3 primary speakers.
  • Start 12-Min Prep Timer: Press "Start Prep Timer" on the front screen. The schedule matrix displays all 8 speaking turns.
  • Guide the A-R-E-I Method:
    • Assertion: What is your team's core claim?
    • Reasoning: Why is this logically true?
    • Evidence: Real Rajshahi context (e.g. RU, Padma, easy-bikes, mango farming).
    • Impact: How does this help/harm students?
  • Bell at 2:00 Remaining: Chime warns teams to finalize speaker handoffs.
🎙️ Phases 3 & 4: Live Stage & Scoring (35m)
  • Order of Speeches: 1. PM (Govt) → 2. LO (Opp) → 3. Minister (Govt) → 4. Deputy (Opp) → 5. Govt Whip → 6. Opp Whip → 7. Opp Reply → 8. Govt Reply.
  • Bells & POI Discipline:
    • 0:00–1:00: Protected time (No POIs). Single bell at 1:00.
    • 1:00–3:00: POI Window OPEN! Opponents stand and say "On that point, Madam Speaker". Max 15s.
    • 3:00–4:00: Final min protected. Single bell at 3:00.
    • 4:00: Double bell (Time up! 20s grace period).
  • Live Rubric Scoring (Teacher): Adjust the 5 sliders (Logic 35, Evidence 20, Delivery 20, Rebuttal 15, Team 10) during each speech.
  • Announce Winner & Best Speaker: Reveal Phase 4 scorecard and deliver 3 minutes of constructive feedback.

⚠️ Classroom Friction Points Encountered & Teacher Mitigations

During the iterative design of this application, several practical friction points arose between digital UI features and real-world classroom dynamics. Below are the key findings and how the streamlined UI resolves them:

1. Multi-Device Login / Room Friction vs Single Smart-TV
The Problem: When requiring all 35 students to scan a QR code, join isolated rooms, and log into phone workspaces, ~5–10 minutes of class time were lost to Wi-Fi connectivity, phone battery issues, and students getting lost across tabs.
The Solution: Reverted to the Single-Master Screen Projector Model. The front screen runs the entire structure (timings, bells, queue). Students turn their desks and collaborate face-to-face with pens, physical placards, and eye contact.
2. On-Screen Scratchpads & Anti-Cheating Insecurity
The Problem: When digital textboxes or scratchpads were projected on the front screen during prep time, teams worried the opposing side would read their arguments or that typing distracted from vocal debate prep.
The Solution: Scratchpads were completely removed from the main screen. Instead, the Prep Room displays the Full 8-Turn Speaking Order Matrix & Exact Durations, giving students a high-level roadmap to structure their own physical notes.
3. Sound Output & Speaker Bell Audibility in Noisy Classrooms
The Problem: Web Audio API bells playing through laptop speakers are often drowned out by excited students arguing during POI windows.
The Solution: Connect the classroom laptop/Smart-TV to the room PA speaker. If audio is quiet, the UI provides high-contrast visual cues: the stage clock turns cyan during open POIs and pulses yellow/red at the 1-minute warning.
4. Adjudication Slowness Between Back-to-Back Speeches
The Problem: Teachers who wait until after the speech to score struggle to recall specific point breakdowns while introducing the next debater.
The Solution: Sliders are built directly on the live stage view. The teacher adjusts sliders in real-time during the speech. Tapping "Next Speaker" automatically archives the current score and loads the next speaker without page reloads.
5. Reply Speech Order Confusion
The Problem: Students often mistakenly assume Government speaks first in reply speeches because they spoke first in substantive turns.
The Solution: The UI enforces authentic Bangladesh parliamentary rules: Turn 7 is automatically locked to Opposition Reply, followed by Turn 8 Government Reply, preventing order mistakes.
