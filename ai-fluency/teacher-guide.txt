AI FLUENCY LAB — VISUAL TEACHING GUIDE
B1–B2 · one 45-minute workflow per lesson · optional Bangla support

TEACH FIRST: 5 MINUTES
Open Start here. Six short slides show:
1. The deliverable: an unchecked and a better example.
2. Delegation: Human / AI draft / Human + AI.
3. Description: Product = what to make; Process = how to work; Performance = how AI behaves as a collaborator.
4. Discernment: skim headings, scan for a detail, then compare the whole claim.
5. Diligence: protect information, explain assistance, check before use.
6. Deliverables: a clear target, a better AI message, and a spoken explanation.

THE READING DEMONSTRATION
Press Watch the steps. The document view changes depth; attention moves from headings to a detail and then to the source/output mismatch. Pause, replay, or choose each step manually. Reduced-motion users get the same steps without camera motion. Skimming and scanning locate evidence; they do not verify meaning on their own. Read the action, condition, relationship, and surrounding meaning closely.

GUIDED ACTIVITIES: 40 MINUTES
1. See the goal (3m): choose a visible output, then build three success criteria. No blank writing box.
2. Place the jobs (4m): put a short task under Human, AI draft, or Human + AI. Feedback explains the division.
3. Build the brief (5m): choose Product, Process, and Performance instructions. The three-part message appears in the simulated chat. Explore all three interface styles.
4. Compare evidence (7m): skim, scan, tap an output phrase, and classify it as supported, changed meaning, or no evidence. Correct checks expose the exact contrast and build a spoken explanation.
5. Repair and say it (5m): choose a factual correction; arrange four sentence chunks; listen and say the result.
6. Check before use (3m): resolve a privacy, transparency, and release decision. The document case stays on HOLD until missing details are confirmed.
7. New situation (8m): guided choices build a new brief. An optional editable sentence appears only after successful practice.
8. Your results (5m): use the built explanation, point to evidence, swap speakers, and download the learning record.

PAIR ROUTINE
A points and chooses. B checks and says why. Swap roles at the next station. Begin with pointing and selection, then rehearse the visible sentence, then try saying it while looking away. Avoid requiring an open-ended written explanation before the learner has enough language and a concrete example.

FEEDBACK
An activity stamp records completion, not proficiency. Wrong choices give a brief retry cue; they do not deduct points. Spoken-practice buttons are self-report. Device speech is optional and may be unavailable. No microphone recording or automatic speaking assessment occurs. Supported claims may be checked at any matching phrase; changed claims accept alternative valid error locations.

ASSESS TWO THINGS SEPARATELY
Each criterion: 0 not demonstrated; 1 with support; 2 independently with evidence.
AI /8: sensible division; clear brief; evidence-based checking/revision; responsibility before use.
English /8: accurate vocabulary; connected reason; useful question/response; a revision preserving meaning.
Use observed speech and the visible result. Do not equate activity completion with mastery.

CLASSROOM USE
Classroom mode enlarges teaching content. The onboarding deck and activity steps are controllable rather than timed slides. The teacher can pause and ask learners to point, predict, compare, and explain. One workflow per lesson. Use a station URL to direct phones; there is no live synchronisation.

WORKFLOW: BUILD AN HONEST CV
Goal: a one-page CV for a local employer.
English: Describe achievements accurately

VISIBLE EXAMPLES
Unchecked: NUSRAT JAHAN | Award-winning graduate | Led 45 volunteers | Improved marks by 40%
Better: NUSRAT JAHAN | English student · graduation 2027 | Tutored 3 school students | Checked in 45 attendees

GOOD RESULT — THREE CRITERIA
True facts: 45 attendees → 45 attendees
Relevant experience: Tutoring → student communication
One readable page: Short headings + clear bullets

DELEGATION KEY
Confirm her experience → Human. Nusrat knows what she did.
Suggest clearer wording → AI draft. AI can suggest words.
Review wording against the facts → Human + AI. AI suggests; Nusrat checks and decides.

THREE-P BRIEF
Product: Draft a one-page CV.
Process: Ask about missing facts first.
Performance: Be an honest editor.

EVIDENCE PRACTICE
1. EVENT EXPERIENCE
Source: Checked in 45 attendees
Output: Led a team of 45 volunteers
Check: changed — Same number. A different job.
Acceptable anchors: Led a team of; volunteers
Correction: Checked in 45 attendees.

2. TUTORING
Source: Tutored 3 school students
Output: Tutored 3 school students
Check: same — The work and number match.
Acceptable anchors: any matching phrase
Correction: Tutored 3 school students.

3. TUTORING RESULTS
Source: No measured result recorded for tutoring
Output: Improved marks by 40% through tutoring
Check: missing — 40% has no supporting evidence.
Acceptable anchors: by 40%
Correction: Remove the 40% claim.

SENTENCE TO BUILD
Tutored 3 school students in English from January to June 2026.
Teacher language note: Choose a precise past-tense verb + object + context. A stronger sentence gives clearer evidence, not a bigger achievement.

DILIGENCE KEY
Passport scan in the prompt → Use experience + placeholders
“AI wrote nothing” → Describe the editing honestly
Email and phone still blank → Add and check them yourself

TRANSFER
New audience: a summer programme abroad.
Target: 70-word teamwork profile
Include: 6-slide group project
Avoid: Invented IELTS score
Check: Match every claim to her facts.
Built sentence: We need a 70-word profile showing real teamwork.

OPTIONAL LESS-SUPPORTED EXTENSION
Nusrat now considers a fictional international summer programme. Its brief values collaboration and asks for a 70-word profile, not a full CV. AI editing is allowed. Use the same facts. Decide what to keep, write a new brief, and say how you would check the result.
One defensible approach: I would confirm the programme requirements myself and ask AI to suggest a 70-word profile using only the fact sheet. I would highlight the group presentation and tutoring, check that “collaborated” does not become “led,” and keep a record of editing. I would not add an IELTS score.

WORKFLOW: COMPARE REAL OPPORTUNITIES
Goal: an evidence-based comparison for Rafi.
English: Distinguish evidence, inference, and uncertainty

VISIBLE EXAMPLES
Unchecked: BEST OPTION: ABROAD | All costs paid! | 85% chance of selection | No need to check dates
Better: OPPORTUNITY CHECK | A · July · eligible | No fee; travel costs unknown | B · June · not eligible

GOOD RESULT — THREE CRITERIA
Source-backed claims: Notice A → column A
Check the dates: Exams ↔ programme dates
Show unknown costs: Not stated → ask

DELEGATION KEY
Set budget and priorities → Human. Rafi sets his limits.
Make a comparison table → AI draft. AI can organise supplied facts.
Check eligibility and choose → Human + AI. AI compares; Rafi verifies and decides.

THREE-P BRIEF
Product: Compare dates, costs and eligibility.
Process: Use the notices; label missing facts.
Performance: Be cautious about unknowns.

EVIDENCE PRACTICE
1. FUNDING
Source: Participation fee waived Travel conditional
Output: All costs paid Travel guaranteed
Check: changed — A fee waiver is not all costs.
Acceptable anchors: All costs; Travel guaranteed
Correction: Fee waived; travel support is conditional.

2. NOTICE A · DATES
Source: Internship 1–30 July No fee
Output: Internship 1–30 July No fee
Check: same — The dates match the notice.
Acceptable anchors: any matching phrase
Correction: Keep 1–30 July.

3. SELECTION
Source: Selection chance not stated in either notice
Output: Selection chance 85% for Rafi
Check: missing — No source gives 85%.
Acceptable anchors: 85%
Correction: Selection chance: not stated.

SENTENCE TO BUILD
According to notice A, there is no participation fee, but travel is not reimbursed.
Teacher language note: Use “according to” for attribution, “may” for possibility, and “not stated” for missing evidence. Do not hedge a clear rule: B explicitly requires third year.

DILIGENCE KEY
Only an AI summary available → Check the organiser’s notice
Some costs not stated → Label them as unknown
Rafi must choose → Explain the trade-offs to him

TRANSFER
New option: an online teamwork project.
Target: Online · BDT 2,000 · 5–25 July
Include: All university years eligible
Avoid: Assume internet is free and reliable
Check: Ask whether meetings must be live.
Built sentence: The online project may fit, but we must check the connection needs.

OPTIONAL LESS-SUPPORTED EXTENSION
A new fictional notice says: “Online teamwork project, 5–25 July; all university years eligible; BDT 2,000 participation fee; internet access required.” Rafi has BDT 8,000 but an unreliable connection. Compare it with A. Write one cautious recommendation and two questions to verify.
One defensible approach: The online project meets the stated year rule and does not overlap exams. The BDT 2,000 fee is within the budget, but the total cost and connection requirements need checking. I would ask whether meetings are live and whether mobile data is enough. I would compare teamwork duties with A before recommending one.

THREE-P BRIEF
Product: Draft a short placement enquiry.
Process: Use my facts; ask what is missing.
Performance: Use polite, natural English.

EVIDENCE PRACTICE
1. EXPERIENCE
Source: Course project: interviewed 6 classmates No publication
Output: Published research: interviewed 6 classmates Expert researcher
Check: changed — A class project became a publication.
Acceptable anchors: Published research:; Expert researcher
Correction: For a course project, I interviewed 6 classmates.

2. AVAILABILITY
Source: Remote 4 hours a week in July
Output: Remote 4 hours a week in July
Check: same — This is the time he can offer.

WORKFLOW: VIBE-CODE A TOOL YOU CAN TEST
Goal: a tested cost calculator for student organisers.
English: Specify behaviour with if–then conditions

VISIBLE EXAMPLES
Unchecked: WORKSHOP COST | 600 + (400 ÷ 5) | BDT 680 each | 0 people → Infinity
Better: WORKSHOP COST | Total costs: BDT 1,000 · 5 people | Correct grouping: (600 + 400) ÷ 5 | BDT 200 each

GOOD RESULT — THREE CRITERIA
Correct calculation: (600 + 400) ÷ 5 = 200
Useful error messages: 0 people → ask for at least 1
Only needed inputs: Costs + people · no personal data

DELEGATION KEY
Decide the formula and expected result → Human. Arif defines what correct means.
Draft a small calculator → AI draft. AI can write a bounded first version.
Test and improve the calculator → Human + AI. AI helps fix; Arif verifies behaviour.

THREE-P BRIEF
Product: Build a phone-friendly cost calculator.
Process: Confirm the formula, then test edge cases.
Performance: Explain assumptions in plain English.

EVIDENCE PRACTICE
1. NORMAL TEST
Source: (600 + 400) ÷ 5 should give 200
Output: 600 + (400 ÷ 5) actually gives 680
Check: changed — Expected 200. Actual 680.
Acceptable anchors: 600 + (400 ÷ 5); 680
Correction: Divide the whole sum by attendees.

2. ROUNDING TEST
Source: 1000 ÷ 3 round up 334
Output: 1000 ÷ 3 round up 334

Acceptable anchors: All cases
