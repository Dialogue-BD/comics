Culture in four rings

You can only see the outside ring. Every ring inside it has to be asked for — and then checked against three people, because one person’s answer is a story, not a culture.

  • Behaviour — what is done? The outside ring, and the only one you can watch.
  • Values — what is good or best? What they put first.
  • Beliefs — what is true? What has to be true for that value to make sense.
  • Worldview — what is real? The shape of the world those beliefs assume.

For the teacher

A four-panel lesson, then four steps: about 35 minutes. The lesson is shown once and the browser remembers; “Show the lesson” brings it back for a new class. Its second panel is the one that matters — the same three values ranked two ways — and it is worth stopping on.

Pairs work well: one student runs the audio, the other keeps notes on the printed field sheet. The three wrong sentences in step 4 are the three traps — restatement, judgment and stereotype — and they are worth more discussion than the three right ones.

Step 3 is the language work. Each interview can be heard three times and the word field changes every time, so a second listen is not the first one with the answers known. Nothing is locked: the class can move on whenever you want them to, and the step simply shows how many of the three have been heard. After a second listen each informant gets a “See the words” button on their own card; the switch under all three still opens every transcript at once for a weaker group. Every underlined word in a transcript can be tapped for its meaning.
