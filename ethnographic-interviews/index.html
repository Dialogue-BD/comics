Culture in four rings

You can only see the outside ring. Every ring inside it has to be asked for — and then checked against three people, because one person’s answer is a story, not a culture.

  • Behaviour — what is done? The outside ring, and the only one you can watch.
  • Values — what is good or best? What they put first.
  • Beliefs — what is true? What has to be true for that value to make sense.
  • Worldview — what is real? The shape of the world those beliefs assume.

For the teacher

A four-panel lesson, then four steps: about 35 minutes. The lesson is shown once and the browser remembers; “Show the lesson” brings it back for a new class. Its second panel is the one that matters — the same three values ranked two ways — and it is worth stopping on.

Pairs work well: one student runs the audio, the other keeps notes on the printed field sheet. The three wrong sentences in step 4 are the three traps — restatement, judgment and stereotype — and they are worth more discussion than the three right ones.

Step 3 is the language work. Each interview can be heard three times and the word field changes every time, so a second listen is not the first one with the answers known; step 3 will not release the class until all three have been heard once. Opening the transcripts releases it — use that for a weaker group, and note that every underlined word there can be tapped for its meaning.

Print the field sheet and the class can run a real interview outside the room with the same four steps. The paper version has no answers in it.

Step 5

Fill the Rings

One ring at a time. Never jump from the outside straight to the centre.

Their rings

1 · Behaviour — the outside ring what is done?

2 · Values what is good or best?

3 · Beliefs what is true?

4 · Worldview — the centre what is real?

Your rings, same situation

1 · Behaviour — the outside ring what is done?

2 · Values what is good or best?

3 · Beliefs what is true?

4 · Worldview — the centre what is real?

Three traps

  • Restatement — you renamed the behaviour and called it a value. Nothing was explained.
  • Judgment — you wrote how you feel about them, not what they rank highest.
  • Stereotype — it explains everything, predicts nothing, and no interview can check it.
Write both sets of rings so that a person from that culture would read yours and say: yes — that is what I was doing.
