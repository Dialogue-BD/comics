One morning, he finally came to his senses. He thought about his father’s house.
Even the people who work for my father have more than enough to eat, he thought. And here I am, starving.
He washed his face at a hand pump. He could no longer pretend that everything was fine.
I’ll go home, he decided. I’ll tell my father I’ve sinned against God and against him.
I’m no longer worthy to be called his son anymore. Perhaps he’ll let me work for him instead.
He set off with very little. Along the way, he took odd jobs in the fields.
Sometimes a truck gave him a ride. Often, he walked barefoot, carrying his shoes.
At last, the familiar fields came into view. Then he saw his father’s house.
