# Expectations & scope

Reporting demands, scope creep, and the 2:17 AM client — the conversations that burn freelancers out.

### "I haven't heard from you, so I have to cancel the order"

**Voice:** A Fiverr buyer, via freelancer Hridoy Hossain's screenshot shared with The Business Standard, circa July 2024
**The words:**
> "I need a quick modification and I see that the days of unavailability are getting longer. I haven't heard from you, so I have to cancel the order."
**Context:** A buyer cancelling not over quality but over silence — days with no word read as "gone," and the order dies before any explanation is possible.
**Classroom use:** The trust cost of silence: draft the one message a freelancer sends *before* disappearing — even bad connectivity gets a warning line.

### The full-time reporting officer

**Voice:** Young Bangladeshi digital marketer, MetaGrow Agency Facebook reel, Oct 7, 2026
**The words (paraphrased):** Hired to run marketing; the client starts demanding second-by-second screenshots. Phone lighting up at 2:17 AM with urgent messages from clients in the USA *and* Bangladesh. Endless "why are the messages low?" and "why no sales?" — "I'm not a marketer anymore, I'm a full-time reporting officer." Small business owners think marketing is magic; "this is the process."
**Context:** Gallows humor about the real disease: expectations were never set, so the client fills the silence with anxiety and the freelancer fills the gap with midnight messages.
**Classroom use:** Students write the kickoff message that prevents this client from existing: reporting cadence, timelines, what month one actually looks like.

### The undiscussed expectation

**Voice:** @sumerausman02, Medium guide "How to Work With Clients Around the World," Aug 2026
**The words (paraphrased):** Most freelancing problems are expectation problems — "the expectation was never discussed."
**Context:** The one-sentence diagnosis behind the reporting-officer trap.
**Classroom use:** The Expectation Conversation — the exact English phrases for setting cadence, scope, and timelines at kickoff.

### "We thought revisions were included"

**Voice:** Freelancing advice creator Jamie Brindle, Facebook reel skit, Aug 30, 2026 (3K likes; comments from freelancers)
**The words (paraphrased):** Client: "We thought revisions were included." Freelancer: "They were. You used them. So what happens now? Now they cost money." One commenter: "I gave them 3 extra revisions — I was shy and didn't know my worth."
**Context:** The exact standoff Bangladeshi freelancers dread — the boundary conversation most can't afford to have, because "included revisions" was never defined.
**Classroom use:** Role-play the reply: one calm sentence that names the limit, prices the extra, and keeps the client.

### "Just" — the word that hides a new job

**Voice:** @fastdigitizer, Medium essay "The Client Red Flags I Learned to Spot After 10 Years of Freelancing," Sep 2026
**The words:**
> "The tell is the word 'just'. Just make the text a bit bigger. Just swap this part out. Just add the second line. Each 'just' is free. Four of them is a second job."
**Context:** A veteran's field rule: the "small change" that replaces the artwork and arrives framed as a correction to the freelancer's error rather than a change to the client's mind.
**Classroom use:** Students circle every "just" in real client messages and draft the sentence that converts it into a billable change order.
