# Bad news & conflict

Delivering bad news, handling disputes and bad reviews — the conversations freelancers dread most.

*Still collecting: a freelancer telling a client results are down, a dispute over revisions.*

### The client who went from "happy to pay" to "not a dime"

**Voice:** Upwork freelancer's BBB complaint, Feb 2026
**The words (paraphrased):** The client agreed in writing before delivery — "Happy to pay you for it." After delivery: "I am not paying you a dime," plus profanity and threats of a negative review. Upwork confirmed the client violated its terms and paid the freelancer $100 from its own fund — but left the retaliatory feedback standing. The freelancer's Job Success Score fell from 100% to 86%, the Top Rated badge was lost, and inbound inquiries dropped from ~78 a day to zero.
**Context:** The documented worst case of feedback-as-weapon: the platform sided with the freelancer on the violation and still let the punishment stand.
**Classroom use:** Dispute language — how to write a calm, evidence-backed platform complaint; and what "winning the case but losing the profile" teaches about keeping everything in writing.

### The cancellation that arrived as silence

**Voice:** Hridoy Hossain, quoted in The Business Standard, July 2024 blackout coverage
**The words (paraphrased):** With Bangladesh offline, Hridoy couldn't reach his clients. One cancelled with a note: a quick modification was needed, the unavailability was getting longer, "I haven't heard from you, so I have to cancel the order." Three running gigs cancelled; his Fiverr rank lost.
**Context:** Bad news delivered *to* the freelancer — but the lesson runs both ways: in a crisis, silence reads as disappearance. His peers' thread lists thousands of dollars in cancelled contracts.
**Classroom use:** The emergency message — how to tell a client bad news (delay, outage, missed deadline) before they find out from the silence.

### The chargeback he won with proof

**Voice:** Muhammad Nayem, Bangladeshi SEO/AEO freelancer, Medium, Jul 3, 2026
**The words (paraphrased):** A client opened a chargeback dispute. Nayem: "I trusted my work, provided proper proof, and WON the case." Order protected, payment secured, reputation maintained.
**Context:** The counterpoint entry — a dispute handled with documentation instead of anger, and the proof-first habit that makes it possible.
**Classroom use:** Building the evidence file as you work — screenshots, delivery records, written agreements — so a dispute is a folder, not a fight.

### Reviews destroyed by deliberate cancellations

**Voice:** Experienced Bangladeshi freelancer, quoted in Prothom Alo, Aug 2026 (Betopia coverage)
**The words (paraphrased):** Getting a gig to Fiverr's first page is "almost like winning a war" — and a career built over five to seven years with thousands of positive reviews "can be destroyed by a few deliberate cancellations." Months to recover a lost ranking.
**Context:** From the Betopia fallout — fake-order manipulation that got real freelancers' work cancelled. The damage here isn't a client's words; it's the rating damage itself.
**Classroom use:** Reputation as inventory — why rating damage is a business risk, and the professional English of asking a client to reconsider a review.

### The platform's own reply script

**Voice:** Fiverr's official blog, "Professional Responses for Every Fiverr Situation"
**The words (paraphrased):** Fiverr's template for answering a bad review: apologize for the disappointment, name what was agreed ("we agreed I would do X"), show what was delivered, ask for a chance to tweak the work — and if not, ask what to do better next time. Their hard rule: never offer free work for a better review; screenshot and report buyers who demand it.
**Context:** The industry's own model answer — useful precisely because it's the bland, safe register freelancers can upgrade from.
**Classroom use:** Rewrite-the-template exercise: students take this safe corporate version and make it sound like a person, keeping the four moves (acknowledge, evidence, offer, learn).
