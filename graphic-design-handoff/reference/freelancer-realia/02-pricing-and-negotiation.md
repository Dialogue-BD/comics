# Pricing & negotiation

Quoting a number, surviving the pushback, saying no — in English, without apologizing.

### The rate card

**Voice:** Ibrahim Niloy, Dhaka digital marketer, Instagram post, Oct 6, 2026
**The words:**
> "কম বাজেটে না, সঠিক দামে কাজ করুন" — don't work on low budget, work at the right price.
**Context:** His card contrasts $5/project gigs against $25 standard and $50+ "best fit" work, urging everyone to set a minimum rate.
**Classroom use:** Students build their own minimum-rate card and defend it out loud.

### Street-food budget

**Voice:** Abul Kalam Azad (A.K Creative Studio), video editor, Instagram reel, Oct 2, 2026
**The words (paraphrased):** Bangladeshi clients want Netflix-level editing on a "street food" budget — then complain your portfolio isn't impressive enough.
**Context:** The line of the month on freelancer Instagram. The real offers behind it, shared in the VideoEditorsBDC Facebook group (Sep 2026): 40 taka a minute for long videos, 200 taka for a "professional" edit, 5,000 taka a month to edit 60 videos.
**Classroom use:** Role-play the reply to a 200-taka "professional edit" offer — in professional English, bridge unburned.

### The fear of raising prices

**Voice:** Aarav Singh, freelancer, Instagram reel, Aug 12, 2026
**The words (paraphrased):** Kept a monthly rate too low for months because raising it felt like watching the client walk off to someone cheaper.
**Context:** Names the emotional block plainly — it's not math, it's fear.
**Classroom use:** Script the rate-increase email: firm, warm, no apology.

### Stop discounting, start negotiating

**Voice:** Shimul Rana, freelancer, Facebook reel, Oct 7, 2026
**The words (paraphrased):** A monologue on client-conversation mistakes — underpricing work and avoiding hard talks; he learned to negotiate price honestly instead of discounting.
**Context:** A before/after story in one minute: discounting felt safe and cost him; honest negotiation felt scary and paid.
**Classroom use:** Pair with Aarav's reel — two freelancers, same fear, different endings.

### What $5/hour teaches

**Voice:** @helenachy_freelance, Threads, Oct 3, 2026
**The words (paraphrased):** Pricing at $5/hour teaches clients to undervalue the work; commenters debate newcomers dumping prices for first reviews.
**Context:** The race-to-the-bottom argued in real time, with working freelancers pushing back on beginners.
**Classroom use:** The "bazaar vs. boardroom" framing — when haggling helps, when it signals you don't believe your own price.

### The call as a negotiation ambush
